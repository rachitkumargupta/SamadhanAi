
DOCUMENT_ID: DRAFT-SEVERITY-001
TITLE: Generic Municipal Complaint Severity Classification
DOCUMENT_STATUS: DRAFT - NOT OFFICIAL MUNICIPAL POLICY
JURISDICTION: Generic municipal prototype
VERSION: 1.0

PURPOSE

This document provides provisional severity-classification criteria
for Samadhan AI Agent 2. It is intended for prototype testing and
must not be represented as an official municipal policy.

Severity is evaluated separately for each distinct issue in a complaint.
The agent must consider the reported facts, potential consequences,
available policy evidence, and uncertainty.

SEVERITY LEVELS

LEVEL: LOW

Use as a provisional classification when the reported issue appears
to have limited impact, no identified immediate safety concern, and
no evidence of substantial service disruption.

Examples for prototype testing:
- A minor non-urgent maintenance concern.
- A localized issue with limited reported impact.

Do not classify an issue as low merely because the complaint contains
few details.

LEVEL: MEDIUM

Use as a provisional classification when the issue has a noticeable
effect on a service, public area, or a limited group of residents,
but the available facts do not establish a significant immediate hazard.

Examples for prototype testing:
- A localized drainage problem.
- A service interruption affecting a limited area.

LEVEL: HIGH

Use as a provisional classification when the reported facts indicate
substantial service disruption, a potentially serious public impact,
significant infrastructure damage, or a serious hazard requiring
prompt assessment.

Examples for prototype testing:
- A major water leak affecting a public area.
- Extensive waterlogging disrupting access.
- Significant damage to public infrastructure.

These examples are not automatic official severity assignments.
The agent must evaluate the available facts and applicable rules.

LEVEL: CRITICAL

Use only when the available evidence supports an immediate or
extreme threat to life, serious injury, or a comparably severe
consequence under the applicable classification policy.

Examples for prototype testing:
- A credible report of an immediately dangerous electrical hazard.
- A serious infrastructure failure presenting an immediate threat.

A critical classification is a recommendation, not an emergency
dispatch action or an official municipal determination.

UNKNOWN OR INSUFFICIENT INFORMATION

If the evidence does not support a defensible severity classification:

- Set severity to undetermined.
- Explain what information is missing.
- Set requires_human_review to true.
- Do not invent facts or assume that an unreported hazard exists.
- Do not treat missing details as proof that an issue is harmless.

ASSESSMENT FACTORS

Evaluate the following factors when relevant:

1. SAFETY
   Is there evidence of a risk of injury, electrocution, contamination,
   or another potential threat to people?

2. EXTENT
   Is the issue localized, widespread, or affecting multiple locations?

3. SERVICE IMPACT
   Is an essential service interrupted or significantly degraded?

4. INFRASTRUCTURE IMPACT
   Is public infrastructure damaged or potentially failing?

5. EXPOSURE
   Are pedestrians, residents, workers, or other members of the public
   potentially exposed to the reported hazard?

6. UNCERTAINTY
   Are important facts missing, contradictory, or unverified?

Do not infer the size of the affected area, number of residents,
or severity of damage unless supported by the complaint or evidence.

POTENTIAL IMMEDIATE HAZARDS

When a complaint reports a potentially immediate hazard:

- Flag it for urgent human attention.
- Explain the reported hazard and why it may require urgent assessment.
- Do not delay the hazard flag merely because an official SLA is missing.
- Do not invent a response deadline.
- Do not assume that a potential-hazard flag automatically establishes
  an official municipal severity category.

For exposed electrical wires, advise people to stay away and contact
the appropriate emergency or utility authority through verified local
channels. Do not advise untrained people to handle the wires.

MULTIPLE ISSUES

Assess each issue independently.

For a complaint containing a major water leak and an exposed electrical
wire, create or recommend separate child tasks if appropriate.

Do not automatically assign both issues the same severity.

A potential electrical hazard may require urgent human attention even
when its official severity category or responsible authority remains
unconfirmed.

EVIDENCE AND DECISION RULES

- Use retrieved policy evidence to apply official classification rules.
- Clearly distinguish official rules from prototype draft criteria.
- Never claim that these draft levels are statutory or municipality-approved.
- Do not invent SLA deadlines from severity labels.
- Do not use model confidence alone as proof that a classification is correct.
- If official severity rules are unavailable, identify the output as a
  provisional prototype recommendation and request human review where needed.
- If the facts are insufficient, use undetermined rather than guessing.

OUTPUT EXPECTATIONS

For each issue, Agent 2 should provide:

- Issue ID.
- Recommended severity.
- Explanation based on reported facts.
- Supporting policy evidence, if available.
- Confidence score.
- Whether human review is required.
- Reason for human review, when applicable.

LIMITATIONS

This document does not establish official municipal response times,
statutory obligations, department jurisdiction, or escalation deadlines.

All severity criteria must be verified against applicable official
policies before the system is used for operational municipal decisions.

END OF DOCUMENT
