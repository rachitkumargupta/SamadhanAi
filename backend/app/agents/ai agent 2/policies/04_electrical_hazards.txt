
DOCUMENT_ID: DRAFT-ELECTRICAL-001
TITLE: Electrical Hazards in Public Areas
DOCUMENT_STATUS: DRAFT - NOT OFFICIAL MUNICIPAL POLICY
JURISDICTION: Generic municipal prototype
VERSION: 1.0

PURPOSE

Provide provisional guidance for identifying, prioritizing, and routing
reports of electrical hazards in public areas.

This document is a project-authored draft, not an official safety
regulation or municipal service-level agreement.

SCOPE

- Exposed or hanging electrical wires.
- Damaged electrical poles or street-lighting equipment.
- Sparking electrical equipment.
- Fallen electrical cables.
- Water near potentially energized electrical equipment.

HAZARD IDENTIFICATION

Extract the reported hazard, location, and available details.

Do not assume a wire is live or safe based solely on its appearance.
Treat a report of an exposed wire as a potential hazard until the
appropriate authority assesses it.

URGENT ATTENTION FLAG

If a complaint reports a potentially immediate electrical hazard:

1. Flag the issue for urgent human attention.
2. Explain the specific reported hazard.
3. Preserve the original issue ID.
4. Recommend contacting the appropriate emergency or electrical utility
   authority through verified local channels.
5. Do not invent contact numbers, dispatch actions, or response deadlines.
6. Do not advise untrained people to touch, move, or repair equipment.

PROVISIONAL SEVERITY GUIDANCE

LOW:
A non-functional street light with no other reported hazard may warrant
a lower provisional severity, subject to the available facts.

MEDIUM:
A damaged public electrical fixture without a reported immediate hazard
may require assessment according to the verified local rules.

HIGH:
A reported electrical fault that may expose people to danger or disrupt
an important public service may warrant a high-severity recommendation.

CRITICAL:
A credible report of an immediate threat, such as a fallen cable in a
public access area, may warrant a critical provisional recommendation
when supported by the reported facts and applicable rules.

These examples are not official municipal severity thresholds.
When evidence is insufficient, use undetermined and request human review.

DEPARTMENT AND AUTHORITY ROUTING

Possible authorities may include an electricity distribution utility,
a municipal street-lighting function, or another infrastructure owner.

These are candidate categories, not confirmed assignments.

Determine responsibility using verified jurisdiction records.
If the responsible authority is unclear, leave the department assignment
unresolved and request human review.

MULTI-ISSUE COMPLAINTS

If an exposed electrical wire is reported alongside a water leak:

- Create or recommend separate child tasks where appropriate.
- Assess the electrical hazard independently.
- Flag the potential electrical hazard for urgent human attention.
- Do not assume both issues belong to the same department.
- Do not delay the safety flag while waiting for jurisdiction evidence.
- Do not infer that an official SLA has started.

SLA AND ESCALATION

This draft defines no official response deadline or escalation procedure.

Only provide an official deadline when a verified, applicable policy
supports that value.

If no applicable SLA is retrieved, set completion_timeline to null.

EVIDENCE REQUIREMENTS

Distinguish verified official policy from draft guidance and unverified
complaint information.

Never represent this document as an official electrical safety code,
municipal regulation, or utility operating procedure.

END OF DOCUMENT
